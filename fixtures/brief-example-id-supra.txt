MEMORANDUM IN SUPPORT OF APPOINTED COUNSEL

I. Counsel at trial

Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (1963), settled the point: "any person haled into court, who is too poor to hire a lawyer, cannot be assured a fair trial unless counsel is provided for him." Id. at 344.

The State's reading would mean that a defendant "can still be assured a fair trial even if counsel is not provided for him." Id.

II. Expenses on a frivolous appeal

Cooter & Gell v. Hartmarx Corp., 496 U.S. 384 (1990), addressed Rule 11. As to appeals, "Rule 38 gives appellate courts ample authority to award expenses." Cooter & Gell, supra, at 406.
